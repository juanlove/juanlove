Motion and Video

The reel above highlights a mix of motion graphics, isometric animation, and video editing from several of my past roles. It includes commercial promotional work from MotionLit, instructional animation produced during my contract with Xpanxion, and tech-focused videography, editing, and motion pieces created at iBUYPOWER.

My Role

Across these projects, I handled the end-to-end creative and technical workflow, including:

  • Concept development
  • Asset design and illustration
  • Motion graphics and animation
  • Compositing and visual effects
  • Video editing and pacing
  • Preparing final outputs for web, social, and marketing use
Key Decisions

Below are a few of the key decisions that shaped how these projects were executed:

  • Built modular motion setups that allowed scenes to be updated and repurposed quickly
  • Designed isometric animations with clean, readable geometry to strengthen instructional clarity
  • Mixed product videography with UI-inspired graphics and motion elements for a modern, tech-driven aesthetic at iBUYPOWER
  • Structured the reel to show a range of animation styles, pacing, and techniques in a cohesive format
Project Outcomes and Tools Used

These projects were created using After Effects for motion and compositing, Illustrator for asset design, Premiere Pro for longer edits, and Photoshop for image preparation. At MotionLit, my promotional animations supported the studio’s marketing efforts and strengthened its public-facing visuals. At Xpanxion, the isometric instructional animations became a reusable system that improved clarity and accelerated production. At iBUYPOWER, I produced motion graphics and edited video assets for product launches, campaigns, and event promotions, giving the marketing team flexible, high-impact visuals tailored for tech and gaming audiences. Additional full-length examples can be viewed in the gallery below.

Print Design

Most of my print work comes from roles where design supported product launches, live events, and large marketing initiatives. This includes brochures, flyers, signage, trade-show materials, packaging-style artwork, and printed assets for conventions and retail use. I’ve also produced instructional print materials—such as PC setup guides and hardware documentation—which required careful layout, hierarchy, and clarity.

My Role

Across all print projects, I handled layout design, typography, asset creation, and preparing files for print production. I created vector illustrations, diagrams, and product visuals in Illustrator and Photoshop, then built final layouts in InDesign. Most print work required balancing brand consistency with readability and adapting design systems to different sizes and formats.

Key Decisions

I structured layouts to remain clear at various scales, standardized templates for recurring event materials, and built modular design systems to reduce design time between product cycles. For instructional materials, I simplified diagrams and used consistent labeling to improve the user experience for audiences with varying technical backgrounds.

Project Outcomes and Tools Used

Print materials were used at events like LVL UP Expo, Anime Expo, and other large gaming conventions, helping unify booth visuals and strengthen brand presence. Instruction manuals improved setup clarity and reduced customer support questions. Most projects were created using Illustrator and Photoshop for asset development and InDesign for multi-page or single-page print layout. More examples are available below.

Multi-Page Layouts

My strongest multi-page example is an admissions packet I redesigned early in my career. The original documents were dense, hard to navigate, and confusing for new students and staff. I rebuilt the entire packet with clear hierarchy, consistent styles, and a layout system that made the information easier to understand at a glance.

My Role

I handled the redesign from start to finish, organizing content, creating typographic styles, and building master pages in InDesign. I also recreated charts, diagrams, and form elements in Illustrator so the packet felt unified and professional across all sections.

Key Decisions

I focused on making the packet easier to use: clearer headings, more readable spacing, visual grouping of related information, and predictable patterns from page to page. The goal was to reduce the “wall of text” feeling and guide readers through each step without confusion.

Project Outcomes and Tools Used

The redesigned packet improved clarity for incoming students and made it faster for staff to walk people through the enrollment process. It set the standard for future printed materials at the college. This project was built primarily in InDesign, with supporting assets created in Illustrator and Photoshop. I’ve also created smaller multi-page technical manuals for product setup and accessory guides, which follow the same structured approach.
